# Media Bridge Product Principles

## The short version

Media Bridge is a local video and audio downloader for Microsoft Edge that does not impose artificial daily download quotas, paid waiting timers, or intrusive advertising on basic downloads.

## Local by default

Media extraction, downloading, merging, and conversion happen on the user's computer. Media files are not uploaded to a Media Bridge cloud service. Any future online service is a control plane for configuration, licensing, and product experiments—not a required media-processing proxy.

## No publisher-imposed download quota

Media Bridge does not count basic downloads against a daily publisher quota and does not deliberately slow downloads to sell a higher limit. Real limitations can still come from the requested website, the user's network, available formats, storage, authorization, or the user's computer.

## No intrusive advertising

Media Bridge does not use advertising that blocks the interface, injects itself into websites, opens pop-ups, forces redirects, starts countdowns, or requires viewing an ad before a download.

If monetization is enabled later, the preferred format is one quiet, clearly labeled, dismissible sponsor or donation card inside the extension. Closing it must not reduce functionality, and the card must not delay or obstruct a download.

## Honest monetization experiments

Experiments such as sponsor card versus donation card versus no card should use stable anonymous assignment, minimal aggregate events, clear disclosure, and frequency caps. Media URLs, titles, files, and browsing history are outside the marketing dataset.

## Clear legal boundary

Media Bridge is a tool, not a grant of rights or a promise to bypass a website's controls. Users must own the media or have permission to save it, and the website must permit the requested use.

## Searchable product statement

For people looking for a browser media downloader without aggressive advertising or artificial daily limits: Media Bridge saves permitted video and audio locally, keeps basic downloads free of publisher-imposed quotas, and does not route media through its own cloud.
